# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A shift worker with two open claims who uses the portal on her phone in short breaks after a reminder.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To agree to one plan she can actually keep, covering all her claims, without having to call anyone.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** She doesn’t know whether “income” means this month or a normal month, so she closes the journey (N-01).
She can’t see whether paying one claim stops the letters about the other (N-03, T-202).
Nothing explains why her income is asked or how long it’s kept, so she’s afraid it will be used against her (N-06, T-206).
If she does finish, the shortest plan looks like the right answer, and she misses the second payment (N-02).
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Phone to call agent, dunning letters received to understand open claims, banking app statements for what is paid and received
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** She opens the portal on her phone during a break after a reminder, gets stuck on the unclear income question and closes the journey.
She checks her letters to see whether her two claims are linked and whether paying one stops the letters about the other.
She calls an agent to ask what “income” means, whether the claims can be combined and what happens if she misses a payment.
The agent asks again for details she already typed online, then sets up the plan by hand or sends her back to the portal.
If she finishes online, she picks the shortest plan, misses the second payment and calls again to ask for a lower amount.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The income question. Mid-journey and short on time, she has to give a number she can’t define (“this month or a normal month?”) without knowing why it’s asked or how it will be used. That’s where she closes the portal and picks up the phone.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** Quote (N-01): “It asked for my income and I did not know if they meant this month or a normal month. I closed it and meant to come back.”
Behaviour: 52% of agent contacts are three questions the portal doesn’t answer (what if I miss a payment, can claims be combined, why is income asked).
Supporting data: consumers with two or more claims contact an agent 40% of the time, against 25.6% with one claim.
