Demystifying the income question

01 / 08
Strategy

We pay twice for one confusing journey

Problem hook

Half of agent contacts after an online plan attempt are questions the portal never answers, and one in five plans consumers agree to breaks within 60 days. We pay twice: in agent time, and again in failed recovery.

Value proposition

A mobile-first instalment journey that answers the three big questions upfront, lets consumers combine their claims into one plan, sizes that plan to what they can keep, and hands the agent a summary when they still call. Success means SSR and the 60-day kept rate rising together, so a higher SSR means better plans, not just more of them.

Initiative hypothesis

For multi-claim consumers like Mara, one combined plan sized to a typical month’s income will raise kept plans per multi-claim starter from 27.4% to 32.4%, while the 60-day missed-payment rate stays at or below 19%. Go/no-go after a 3-month pilot in Germany.

Why now · the SSR trap

SSR counts a plan the moment it’s created. When the plan screen began showing the 6-month plan first, SSR rose from 32.3% to 34.7%, missed payments rose from 15.6% to 19.0%, and kept plans per starter stayed flat at 37.3%.

Who carries the pain

Consumers with 2+ claims are 35.5% of starters but make 47% of agent contacts. Their SSR is 26% with two claims and 19.3% with three or more, against 40.6% with one. Shift workers miss payments 22.5% of the time, against 17.4% on a fixed salary.

Where they leave

Two steps lose 61% of everyone who drops out: the income question (19.3%, 1,421 a month) and the document upload that follows it (22.1%; 853 documents never arrived, T-201).

02 / 08
Research

Mara’s workaround: close the portal, pick up the phone

Persona

Mara, 38 · shift worker · two open claims · uses the portal on her phone in short breaks. Goal: one plan for both claims that she can actually keep, without calling anyone.

Workaround · what she does today
  1. Opens the portal on a break after a reminder, can’t tell which income figure is meant, and closes it.
  2. Checks her paper letters to work out whether her two claims are linked.
  3. Calls an agent to ask what “income” means, whether the claims can be combined and what a missed payment means.
  4. The agent asks for everything again, because he can’t see what she typed online.
  5. If she does finish online, she picks the shortest plan, misses the second payment and calls again.
Core frustration

The income question. Short on time, she has to give a number she can’t define (“this month or a normal month?”) without knowing why it’s asked or how it will be used.

The alternative she picks: a phone call
  • “It asked for my income and I did not know if they meant this month or a normal month. I closed it and meant to come back.” N-01
  • “Honestly I prefer to call. A person can tell me if this is going to cost me more.” N-04
  • Why income is asked: 16.2% of agent contacts, about 12 minutes each.

Why B2 first

It fixes the exact moment Mara gives up (N-01), sits on the 19.3% drop, needs no back-end work (Effort 1), and the clear income figure it produces is the input B3 needs later.

PRD · the four must-haves
  1. Name the period: “Your usual monthly income after tax”, plus “If your pay changes, use a lower month.”
  2. One line on why we ask, next to the field.
  3. One line on who sees it and how long it’s kept, from approved Data Protection text.
  4. All of it readable at 375px without scrolling, because 78% of starters are on mobile.
What the PRD makes explicit

Every line comes from a versioned, approved text ID. If one is missing, the step is blocked and a person is offered. No developer or AI writes the wording, least of all how long her data is kept.

What the prototype revealed: cases the PRD hadn’t covered, such as German number formats (1.850 vs 1,850), a “no income right now” answer that needs a support path, and a retention period only Data Protection can fill in. They’re useful, but they stay out of the A/B variant so the test measures one change.

Validation

First test: does a clear question get more people through?

Test hypothesis

For multi-claim consumers like Mara, a clear income question (which figure, why we ask, who sees it) will raise income-step completion by 5 points, from 80.7% to 85.7%, within 4 weeks, without raising agent contacts or missed payments.

Control (A) · today

The step asks for “income” with no period, no reason and no retention line (T-206). Shift workers can’t tell which month is meant (N-01), and 19.3% leave here.

Variant (B) · one change, four lines of copy
  • Label: “Your usual monthly income after tax”.
  • Variable-pay hint, always visible.
  • Why line (≤20 words) and retention line (≤25 words), from approved text.
  • Nothing else changes: no new answers, no new validation.
Guardrails
  • Agent contacts within 7 days: no more than 2 points above control.
  • 60-day missed payments: a tripwire, not a test. Roll back above control + 3 points or 22%; about 400 agreements per arm can’t detect +1.

06 / 08
Story

What I learned on the way

Aha moment

A plan created is not a case solved.

SSR went up while kept plans stayed flat. So I stopped asking how to get more plans created, and started asking how to get more plans kept.

The SSR trap · before and after the 6-month plan was shown first (Month 4)
Friction points
  • My first hook was about agent cost, with no consumer in it. The data made me rewrite it around Mara.
  • My best metric was too slow. Kept plans take 60 days and depend on later screens, so the B2 test measures the step itself and kept plans protect it.
  • The data shows one segment at a time, so “two claims plus shift work” is still an assumption.
Key takeaways
  1. Fix the one place people leave before building the big thing.
  2. Measure the outcome a metric can’t fake: kept plans, not created plans.
  3. Legal and data-use wording only ever comes from approved text, never from a developer or a model.
What I’d do next
  1. Get the multi-claim baseline, then run the 4-week B2 test.
  2. Revert the 6-month-first plan default.
  3. Build B3, affordability sizing, on the clear income figure.
  4. Then B1, one plan for several claims, to close the initiative.
